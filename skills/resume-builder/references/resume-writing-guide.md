# Maintained resume writing guide

Reviewed 8 October 2026. This is active plugin guidance; the numbered GPT guides remain historical source records.

## Evidence before wording

Keep a compact claims ledger while drafting, without exposing hidden reasoning or adding it to the payload. Track a consequential claim, its candidate-provided source, and whether it is supported, ambiguous, or unsupported. Direct statements and readable resume passages can support facts; a job posting supports employer requirements only. User approval authorizes a transfer, but does not make an invented achievement factual. Resolve contradictions instead of picking the more impressive version.

- Supported: rewrite without expanding seniority, personal ownership, scale, certainty, or result.
- Ambiguous: ask one focused question if it changes the draft materially; otherwise preserve the limited fact and flag it outside the resume.
- Unsupported: omit the claim or offer an explicitly labeled suggestion to discuss. Never convert it into a candidate fact, even to improve keyword coverage.

Distinguish individual work from team outcomes. Retain “contributed,” “supported,” “approximately,” and other meaningful qualifications. Ask whether a number is verified and what it measures; do not derive savings or percentages from incomplete inputs. Preserve official titles and employment type. A target title may appear as an aspiration or headline, not as invented past employment.

## A useful rewrite

Start with the action, then explain what was delivered, how, and why it mattered when known. A factual deliverable or scope is enough when outcomes are unknown. Prefer specific nouns over inflated verbs. Current recurring work normally uses present tense; completed work uses past tense. Do not change the time meaning of a completed project merely because the job is current.

Princeton's [accomplishment-statement guidance](https://careerdevelopment.princeton.edu/resume-guide/action-oriented-accomplishment-statements) supports combining action, context, and result with quantitative or qualitative evidence. This guide's examples and factual guardrails are Infinite Resume editorial choices, not copied candidate content. Source checked 8 October 2026.

Use a brief summary only when it adds useful positioning. Group demonstrated skills for the target audience. Remove repeated duties rather than substituting synonyms. Make technical detail understandable without weakening its meaning. Preserve relevant substantive sections; propose moving or shortening low-relevance content and explain meaningful omissions. Do not silently discard a certification, project, publication, or volunteer role.

## Adapt to the candidate

These are editorial starting points, not universal employer rules. Use the candidate's target role and available facts; ask about one important gap at a time.

| Candidate or target | Evidence to emphasize | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Student or first role | Real projects, coursework, internships, activities, individual contribution and deliverables; education can lead when it is the strongest evidence. | Invented employment, proficiency, graduation dates, project users, or results. |
| Experienced specialist | Relevant recent delivery, tools in context, system/customer/business scope, constraints and actual outcomes; compress older repetition. | Turning participation into ownership or adding every advertised technology. |
| Senior technical or product role | Confirmed design decisions, trade-offs, adoption, operational responsibility, mentoring and collaboration. Ask whether work was led, implemented, or supported. | Equating tenure with leadership or inventing architecture, scale, reliability gains or roadmap authority. |
| Executive or manager | Verified remit, strategy, team scope, decisions and organizational outcomes. Budget, revenue and board exposure belong only when supplied. | Assuming P&L ownership, attributing all team results personally, or exposing confidential figures. |
| Career changer | A clear target, supported transferable evidence and relevant real training/projects. Retain the original employment history and titles. | Renaming an old role to the target occupation or describing learning as professional experience. |
| Nontechnical service, sales or operations | Customer/process context, workload if supplied, accuracy, coordination, training, deliverables and real outcomes. | Invented sales targets, conversion rates, satisfaction scores or tools. |
| Freelance, contract or gap | Clearly label real engagement type; group work only when chronology remains clear. Include a career break or confidential client label only at the user's preference. | Guessing gap reasons, disguising gaps with false dates, revealing clients without permission, or making a contract look like permanent employment. |
| Academic or regulated profession | Clarify whether they need a job resume or an academic CV. Preserve verified credentials, publication attribution and requested specialist sections. | Guessing licenses, registrations, author order, legal eligibility or a universal CV length rule. |

Match the requested language and audience. Ask the destination country only when it changes the guidance; do not impose US resume conventions worldwide. Keep names, titles and credentials accurately represented when translating. Never infer language proficiency or credential equivalence.

Harvard's [resume guidance](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/hes-create-impactful-resumes-and-cover-letters/) supports tailoring to the reader, concise factual language, readable organization and reverse chronology, and notes that country conventions differ. The role-specific choices above extend those principles; they are not claims that every employer prefers a particular layout. Source checked 8 October 2026.

## Factual worked examples

All examples below are invented teaching fixtures. Their listed facts support only their corresponding example. Never copy these employers, numbers, tools or accomplishments into a real candidate's resume. No example needs a fabricated metric to sound useful.

**Technology**

- Supplied fixture facts: implemented retry handling in a Python payment service; added automated tests; no measured reliability result; worked as a contributor.
- Before: “Worked on improving payments.”
- After: “Implemented retry handling in a Python payment service and added automated tests for failed-payment scenarios.”
- Do not add: “Owned payment architecture” or “reduced failures by 40%.”

**Nontechnical operations**

- Supplied fixture facts: scheduled delivery appointments, updated order records and explained delays to customers; no customer-satisfaction measure.
- Before: “Responsible for orders and customer service.”
- After: “Scheduled delivery appointments, maintained order records and explained delivery delays to customers.”
- Do not add: “Achieved 98% customer satisfaction.”

**Student**

- Supplied fixture facts: a course project cleaned a public transit dataset in Python, checked missing values and produced charts; the student did that work personally; no deployed product.
- Before: “Data science project using Python.”
- After: “Cleaned a public transit dataset in Python, checked missing values and created charts to describe service patterns.”
- Do not add: “Launched a transit platform used by thousands.”

**Executive**

- Supplied fixture facts: managed three regional operations leads; coordinated a monthly planning review; each lead contributed to decisions; no supplied financial outcome.
- Before: “Strategic leader driving transformation.”
- After: “Managed three regional operations leads and coordinated monthly planning reviews to align regional priorities.”
- Do not add: “Transformed profitability” or an invented budget.

**Career change**

- Supplied fixture facts: official title Support Analyst; handled onboarding questions in Zendesk, documented recurring issues, and seeks Customer Success roles; no Salesforce or renewals experience.
- Before: “Customer Success Manager with Salesforce expertise.”
- After: “Support Analyst handling customer onboarding questions in Zendesk and documenting recurring issues for the support team.”
- Tailoring note outside the resume: onboarding evidence transfers; Salesforce and renewal ownership remain unverified gaps.

## A low-friction conversation

Provide value before optional intake. Existing material should yield an actual rewrite; missing metrics or contacts must not start a long questionnaire. Ask a useful question only when the answer affects the draft. Show checkpoints after meaningful progress, not on every reply. Once a complete draft is ready, show it once, list material confirmation needs separately, and offer refinement or the connected handoff. Reuse answered preferences and prior exact-draft approval; material revisions require reviewing the revised complete draft before transfer.

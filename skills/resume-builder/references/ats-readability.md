# ATS readability and editorial review

Primary sources checked 8 October 2026. These precautions reduce known risks; they do not establish a universal ATS rule or guarantee selection.

## What can actually be checked

Name the evidence available before reporting findings:

| Available evidence | Supported report | Unsupported claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pasted resume text or a chat draft | Content, headings, chronology, keyword evidence and text organization review. | PDF layout, font size, page count or successful document parsing. |
| A document that the host can inspect | Observed layout and visible readability issues on the inspected pages. State any unreadable or uninspected pages. | Successful extraction or vendor parsing based on appearance alone. |
| Text extracted from the exact document | Compare extracted names, dates, headings, bullets and order against the visible/source content. State missing or scrambled text. | Every ATS will extract or rank the same way. |
| An actual receiving system's parse result | Observed field mapping and errors for that file, system and test. | A universal ATS percentage, screening decision or interview guarantee. |

Use available host document tools only when they can perform the stated check. If extraction, visual inspection or OCR is unavailable, say what remains unchecked and request pasted text or a usable file without pretending a test ran. Keep any OCR uncertainty explicit; never guess an unreadable name, date or qualification. Do not upload candidate data to a third-party scanner without the user's specific instruction and informed destination choice.

## Practical precautions

Greenhouse documents partial or failed parsing from columns, complex tables, graphics, image-only uploads, inconsistent sections, and contact information in headers, footers or text boxes. It lists incomplete job titles as another risk. Recommend clear headings and a simple reading order where the document has those hazards. Preserve factual titles and company names; never invent a legal suffix or seniority to please a parser. Its 2.5 MB limit is Greenhouse-specific. A listed risk is not proof that a particular file fails. See [Greenhouse: unsuccessful resume parse](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse), updated 2 March 2026.

MIT advises checking the employer's accepted file type, using legible common fonts, incorporating relevant terms meaningfully, and inspecting a plain-text version for missing text or incorrect order. Keep supported keywords in context, expand a known acronym when useful, and avoid stuffing or hidden text. A local extraction check is a useful approximation, not the employer's ATS test. Neither PDF nor DOCX is universally best; follow the receiving system's instructions. See [MIT: make your resume ATS-friendly](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/make-your-resume-ats-friendly/).

For Infinite Resume, `classic-ats` is the conservative default. Template choice is not a parser certification. Review the exported document, not only chat text, before commenting on its final layout. In a suggested extraction check, compare contact/name presence, section order, role/employer/date associations, special characters and complete bullets. Report observed problems with their location and a practical repair; flag untested risks separately.

## Evidence-based tailoring

For each important requirement, identify a supported match, transferable evidence, or an unverified gap. A requirement with no candidate evidence stays outside the resume as a question or learning gap. Do not estimate match percentages from keyword overlap. Distinguish parsing problems, editorial weaknesses and missing qualifications; they require different fixes. Without a target role or posting, assess general usefulness and label role-specific alignment unassessed.

## Requested editorial scoring

Score only if the user asks. Say “Infinite Resume editorial rubric; this is a content/readability estimate, not an ATS scan or hiring prediction.” The rubric below is our editorial framework, not a validated vendor measure. Give a brief reason and example for each assessed dimension; use **N/A** when necessary evidence is unavailable, especially document readability from chat text alone. Never score unknown evidence as a failure or pretend an invented claim is acceptable because other dimensions score well.

| Dimension | 0 — material issue | 1 — needs substantial work | 2 — usable, with specific improvements | 3 — strong on available evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Factual fidelity | Invented or contradicted consequential claim. | Ambiguity about role, dates or ownership changes meaning. | Supported facts; a limited ambiguity is explicitly flagged. | Consequential claims trace to supplied facts; qualifiers preserved. |
| Role relevance | Draft misrepresents fit or targets the wrong role. | Relevant evidence is buried or largely absent. | Core relevant evidence is visible; prioritization can improve. | Most useful evidence leads; genuine gaps remain honest. |
| Specific evidence | Unsupported outcomes replace evidence. | Vague duties with little context. | Concrete work, tools or scope; some bullets need detail. | Distinct contributions with relevant scope or verified outcomes. |
| Clarity and voice | Meaning unclear or misleading. | Jargon, repetition or inflated prose obscures work. | Readable and accurate with a few weak phrases. | Precise, natural wording and distinct bullets. |
| Structure and chronology | Roles, dates or credentials conflict materially. | Organization makes history difficult to follow. | Clear sections and history with minor inconsistency. | Easy to skim; sections and chronology accurately organized. |
| Brevity | Crucial evidence lost through truncation. | Length or repetition obscures priorities. | Relevant detail retained with some compression possible. | Detail proportionate to experience and purpose; little duplication. |
| Supported terminology | False qualifications or hidden keyword stuffing. | Supported important terms are missing or detached from work. | Key terms appear accurately; placement can improve. | Supported role language is clear and integrated naturally. |
| Document readability | Observed essential text missing or unusable. | Observed layout/extraction disrupts role or section reading. | Inspected file reads clearly with isolated repairable issues. | Inspected layout and available extraction checks show clear reading order and complete essential content. |

Report dimension results first. If an overall number is requested, total only assessed dimensions as earned points out of `3 × assessed dimensions`; list all N/A dimensions. Do not convert it to an “ATS score.” A material factual conflict still blocks transfer until resolved, regardless of the total. Prioritize three concrete fixes and show at least one factual before/after rewrite instead of ending with a score alone.
